01 — The idea

What Literary is

Literary is a calm, personal reading companion — part shelf, part diary, part librarian. It lets you log what you're reading beautifully, tells you what to read next in your own taste, shows you what the world is reading right now, and teaches you a lovely word each day.

It is deliberately simple and minimalist. No noisy social feed, no gamified streak-shaming, no clutter. The whole app should feel like a well-bound notebook: generous white space, warm paper tones, a single confident accent, and typography that makes you want to linger. Every screen answers one question and answers it clearly.

The people it is for are readers who keep mental lists — the ones with a stack of "someday" books on the nightstand. Literary turns that pile into something you actually enjoy tending.

The Shelf

The heart of the app — logging and tracking, made to feel like arranging real books.

Tend your pile.

Log a book in seconds, then watch your shelf fill

Search a title, tap it, and it lands on a shelf as a real-looking spine with cover art. Every book carries a status — Want to read, Reading, Finished, or Set aside — plus your rating, private notes, favourite quotes, and the dates you started and finished.

  • Three ways to view: a visual bookshelf, a tidy list, or a reading timeline by month.
  • Progress tracking: log current page or percent; a thin brass bar shows how far you are.
  • Quotes & notes: keep the lines worth remembering right on the book's page.
  • Gentle stats: books this year, pages read, genres explored — shown quietly, never as pressure.
  • Book detail page: cover, synopsis, your rating, notes, and "readers who liked this also read…".

05

Discover

Recommendations that actually sound like you — and explain themselves.

A librarian who knows your taste.

Customized picks, with a reason attached

Discover reads your history — the genres, authors, moods and ratings on your shelf — and suggests what to read next. Crucially, every recommendation says why, so it feels like advice from someone who's paying attention, not a black box.

  • Taste profile: built quietly from your ratings, favourite authors, and most-read genres.
  • "Because you loved…": each pick names the book or trait it's based on.
  • Mood shelves: curated rows like Cozy & slow, Big ideas, One-sitting reads, Comfort re-reads.
  • Feedback loop: thumbs, "not for me", and "save for later" refine future picks.
  • Discover a genre: gentle on-ramps into corners you haven't explored yet.
How the engine works (MVP) Start simple and reliable: score candidate books by overlap of genre, author, and "readers-also-liked" signals against your shelf, weighted by your ratings. Later, layer an optional AI pass (an LLM given your top-rated books) to write the one-line "why" and surface less obvious matches.

06

Trending — "The Ledger"

Your page of top reads: what's rising overall, and the leaders inside each genre, drawn from recent articles and buzz.

What the world is reading.

Top reads, overall and by genre

The Ledger is the "pager" you described — a browsable board of trending books. A genre switcher runs across the top (All, Literary, Fantasy, Romance, Mystery, Non-fiction, and more), and each view lists the current top titles with a small movement indicator, so you can see what's climbing.

  • Overall & per-genre boards: one tap swaps the whole ranked list.
  • Sourced from the moment: recent book news, reviews, and bestseller signals — each entry links to why it's trending.
  • Movement markers: ▲ rising, ● steady, NEW just entered.
  • One-tap to shelf: add any trending book straight to Want to read.
  • Weekly "Notable" rail: a small editorial strip of new releases and prize news.
Where the data comes from For a reliable MVP, aggregate from an open source you can query on a schedule — e.g. a books API for popularity/new-release signals plus a news/RSS feed of book coverage — cache it daily, and rank. Present it honestly as "trending this week", refreshed on a cron.

Word of the Day

Its own tab — a small daily ritual that fits a reader, styled to match everything else.

/tsoon-doh-koo/
noun · from Japanese

The habit of acquiring books and letting them pile up, unread, in a comforting, ever-growing stack.

"Her nightstand was a monument to tsundoku — a leaning tower of good intentions she loved all the same."
✶ Save to journal ↔ See yesterday ◷ Word history

Cohesive with the rest of the app

The word lives on the same parchment, uses the same Fraunces display and brass accent, and leans into bookish, literary vocabulary — words about reading, language, and feeling (petrichor, sonder, librocubicularist, bibliosmia) rather than generic dictionary fare.

  • One word a day, with pronunciation, part of speech, origin, definition, and a hand-written example sentence.
  • Save to your Word Journal — a growing personal collection you can revisit.
  • Streak, gently: a quiet count of words collected, never a nag.
  • Optional daily nudge so it becomes a small morning habit.
  • Curated list + fallback: ship a hand-picked word bank; optionally enrich definitions from a dictionary API.

Stats & Goals

A yearly reading goal and a quiet picture of your year in books — celebratory, never a guilt trip.

Your year in reading.

Set a goal, then watch the year fill in

Pick a target for the year and Literary tracks it with a soft progress ring and a gentle "on track" pace — no red streak-shaming, no pressure. Below it, a calm dashboard shows the shape of your reading: how much, how often, and what you gravitate toward.

  • Reading goal: a yearly target with a progress ring and pace ("3 books ahead").
  • Stat tiles: books this year, pages read, average rating, current streak.
  • Books per month and a by-genre breakdown of what you actually read.
  • Year in review: a shareable end-of-year card — favourites, totals, top genre.
  • Tone stays kind: milestones are celebrated; a slow month is never scolded.

Goodreads Import — "The Bridge"

Bring years of reading history across so Literary feels full on day one. It lives in Settings — a one-time action, not a permanent tab.

An honest technical note — read this first Goodreads retired its public API to new developers in December 2020, so a live, real-time Goodreads connection isn't possible to build reliably today. The dependable path — and what nearly every reading app now uses — is Goodreads' own "Export Library" CSV, which every user can download from their account. Design "The Bridge" around that. You can frame it to users as connecting their Goodreads; under the hood it's a clean CSV import.
Ten years of reading, in one file.

How the Bridge works

Export from Goodreads

Guide the user: My Books → Import/Export → Export Library. Goodreads emails/serves a CSV.

Drop it into Literary

A friendly upload zone parses the CSV (title, author, ISBN, your rating, shelves, dates read, review).

Map & enrich

Match Goodreads shelves to Literary statuses; fetch cover art and synopses by ISBN so everything looks right.

Review & confirm

Show a preview — "312 books, 41 with ratings" — let them deselect any, then import. Ratings, reviews and dates carry over.

Build it as a thin layer over Literary's own API once the app exists. Treat the Goodreads side as CSV-in; treat MCP as an assistant bridge into Literary, not out of Goodreads.

Data model & stack

A small, sane schema and a stack that runs cleanly on Replit.

Core tables

// the essentials User { id, name, email, taste_profile } Book { id, title, author, isbn, cover_url, synopsis, genres[], year } ShelfEntry { id, user_id, book_id, status, rating, progress, started_at, finished_at } Note { id, entry_id, kind, body } // note | quote | review ReadingGoal { id, user_id, year, target, progress } WordOfDay { id, date, word, ipa, part_of_speech, origin, definition, example } SavedWord { id, user_id, word_id } TrendingItem { id, book_id, genre, rank, movement, source_url, week }

Suggested stack for Replit

  • FrontReact + Vite · Tailwind for the tokens above
  • BackNode + Express · or Replit's built-in app template
  • DataPostgres · Replit DB or Neon; Prisma ORM
  • AuthReplit Auth · or email magic-link
  • BooksOpen Library / Google Books API · covers, search, ISBN lookup
  • WordsCurated JSON bank · + optional dictionary API
  • JobsDaily cron · rotate word, refresh trending
  • AIOptional LLM · rec "why" lines & taste summary

Paste this into Replit

A single, self-contained build prompt that carries the concept, aesthetic, and features. Copy it straight into Replit's AI agent to scaffold Literary.

replit_build_prompt.txt
Build "Literary" — an aesthetic, minimalist reading tracker + book-discovery web app.

DESIGN
- Feeling: calm, literary, "modern bookbinding"; generous whitespace, one accent. Include a dark mode.
- Palette: parchment #EEE9DB ground, ink-green #182420 text, bottle-green #293C32 surfaces, brass #A2751E accent, oxblood #7C332C rare pop. Dark mode: deep green-black ground, warm ivory text, brighter brass.
- Fonts (Google): Fraunces (headings/wordmark), Newsreader (body), JetBrains Mono (labels/metadata).
- Voice: warm and unhurried. Buttons say what they do. Empty states are gentle invitations.

NAV — 5 tabs (bottom bar on mobile / left rail on desktop): Shelf, Discover, Trending, Word, Stats. Goodreads import is a one-time action, so put it in Settings, not a tab.

FEATURES
1. SHELF (core): search + add books (covers/synopsis via Open Library or Google Books API). Per book: status (Want to read / Reading / Finished / Set aside), star rating, progress (page or %), private notes, saved quotes, start/finish dates. Views: bookshelf, list, monthly timeline. Book detail page with notes + "readers also liked".
2. DISCOVER: personalized recs from the user's shelf (genres/authors/ratings). Every rec shows a one-line reason ("Because you loved X", "Same author"). Mood shelves (Cozy & slow, Big ideas, One-sitting reads). Thumbs feedback refines picks. Scoring: genre/author/"also-liked" overlap weighted by ratings; optional LLM to write the "why".
3. TRENDING ("The Ledger"): ranked board of top books with a genre switcher (All, Literary, Fantasy, Romance, Mystery, Non-fiction…). Each entry shows movement (▲/●/NEW) + source link; one tap adds to Want-to-read. Refresh daily via cron from a books API + book-news feed; label "trending this week".
4. WORD (own tab, same styling): one literary/bookish word daily — word, IPA, part of speech, origin, definition, example sentence (e.g. tsundoku, petrichor, bibliosmia). "Save to journal" builds a Word Journal; gentle streak. Rotate from a curated JSON word bank via daily cron.
5. STATS & GOALS: yearly reading goal with a soft progress ring + "on track" pace. Stat tiles (books this year, pages, avg rating, current streak). Books-per-month chart + by-genre breakdown. Shareable year-in-review card. Celebratory, never punishing.
6. IMPORT ("The Bridge") — in Settings: Goodreads history import. NOTE: Goodreads closed its public API in 2020 — use their CSV export. Flow: user exports library (My Books → Import/Export → Export Library) → upload CSV → parse (title, author, ISBN, rating, shelves, dates, review) → map shelves to statuses → enrich covers by ISBN → preview count → confirm. Carry over ratings, reviews, dates.

DATA: User, Book, ShelfEntry(status,rating,progress,dates), Note(note|quote|review), ReadingGoal(year,target,progress), WordOfDay, SavedWord, TrendingItem(genre,rank,movement,source,week).

STACK: React+Vite+Tailwind (map palette/fonts to theme tokens), Node/Express, Postgres+Prisma, Replit Auth, Open Library/Google Books, daily cron (word + trending). Build Shelf + Word first (MVP), then Stats, Discover, Trending, Import. Keep the aesthetic consistent and responsive across every screen.
